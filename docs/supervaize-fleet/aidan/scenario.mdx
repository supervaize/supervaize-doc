# Write an Aidan scenario

A scenario is the campaign-specific design of an interview. It says why the interview exists, who is interviewed, what must be learned, in which order, and how the conversation ends.

- **It is not a full prompt.** Aidan's tone, listening, probing, safety and technical handling are built in. Leave them out.
- **Start from the outputs.** Every question must serve something the interview has to make knowable.
- **Keep it short and mostly linear.** The limit is 12,000 characters. Aim for 4,000 to 7,000.

:::tip[Drafting with an AI assistant]
Point your assistant to this page when you ask it to draft a scenario.
:::

## Leave platform behaviour out

Do not write instructions about:

- tone of voice, warmth, empathy or conversational style;
- asking one question at a time, listening, or keeping questions concise;
- generic probing, clarification, summaries and transitions;
- adapting language to the participant;
- interruptions, pauses, reconnection or other technical handling;
- standard safety, privacy and consent guardrails;
- generic opening, closing, thanks and feedback.

Repeating them makes the scenario longer, more brittle and more likely to conflict with platform improvements.

Add such an instruction only for a **specific exception**: a mandatory legal disclosure, a forbidden topic, an exact approved phrase. Test with the defaults first, observe a failure, then make the smallest change.

## Start from the required outputs

Before writing questions, list what the interview must establish. For each output, define:

- the field, insight, decision or hypothesis;
- the evidence needed to support it;
- whether it is mandatory or optional;
- what stays confidential, attributable or anonymised.

Remove questions that are merely interesting.

## Build the flow

A default sequence. Short interviews can merge phases.

| Phase | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Context and qualification** | Confirm the participant's role and relevance. Do not ask what is already known. |
| **2. Current reality** | What happens today, through recent, concrete examples. |
| **3. Experience and evidence** | What worked, what failed, how often, with what consequences. |
| **4. Needs, barriers and trade-offs** | Unmet needs, root causes, constraints, competing priorities. |
| **5. Future state or options** | Desired outcomes and candidate changes, only after the current state is clear. |
| **6. Prioritisation** | Force a choice: what matters most, what comes first. |
| **7. Closure** | Check the key interpretation, capture what was missed, state the next step. |

For each section, write an **objective**, the **essential questions** and the **evidence to obtain**. Do not script alternate phrasings. Aidan conducts the conversation.

Good questions follow an evidence path:

- Begin broad enough to avoid imposing your assumptions.
- Move from a general claim to a specific recent example.
- Quantify when useful: frequency, time, cost, volume.
- Ask hypothetical questions only after the facts are established.
- Avoid compound, leading and answer-containing questions.

## Branch deliberately

Add a branch only when an answer should change what happens next. Each branch needs its own end-to-end test.

Write each branch as:

> **Condition** → **Objective** → **Topics or evidence to collect** → **Return point or exit**

Typical branches: the participant has or has not used the product, has direct or second-hand experience, is a decision-maker or a contributor, or is short on time. Every branch rejoins the main flow or defines a clear early exit.

## Control size and time

An oversized scenario cannot be fixed with a pacing instruction. Size it before writing.

| Scenario size | Top-level branches |
| --- | --- |
| Under 6,000 characters | 2 at most |
| 6,000 to 9,000 characters | 3 at most |
| Above 9,000 characters | 4 at most, each one justified |

Classify every section:

- **Must cover**: required for a valid interview. Never skipped.
- **Cover if relevant**: triggered by the participant's context or answers.
- **Optional depth**: only when time remains.

A workable split at 5,500 characters:

| Phase | Share |
| --- | --- |
| Context and qualification | 10% |
| Current reality | 30% |
| Experience and evidence | 25% |
| Needs, barriers, trade-offs | 20% |
| Prioritisation and closure | 15% |

If one phase needs more than 35%, the scenario is trying to be two interviews.

**Estimate duration from dialogue, not question count.** Each substantive theme costs three to five turns. A five-minute interview supports two or three themes.

## Use only the context that exists

Placeholders are replaced at interview time. Use only the ones below.

| Kind | Placeholders |
| --- | --- |
| Workspace and campaign | `{{ tenant.name }}`, `{{ campaign.name }}`, `{{ campaign.caller_name }}` |
| Timing | `{{ interview_date }}`, `{{ session.localtime }}` |
| Participant | `{{ contact.first_name }}`, `{{ contact.email }}`, `{{ contact.city }}`, `{{ contact.country }}`, `{{ contact.timezone }}`, `{{ contact.greeting }}`, `{{ contact.greeting_style }}` |
| Participant metadata | `{{ contact.metadata.<key> }}`, defined per campaign |

Rules:

1. **Never reference a field that is not in the table.** `{{ contact.role }}` or `{{ contact.company }}` do not resolve, and validation blocks the scenario.
2. **Do not write prose placeholders.** "Use the participant's bio as a baseline" makes Aidan invent a bio. If the information is not loaded, ask for it.
3. **Check metadata for every participant.** A key that is filled for most participants passes testing and breaks in production.
4. **Memory of earlier sessions is not guaranteed.** A first interview has none. Any "as you mentioned last time" needs a path for both cases.
5. **Asking costs time.** Collect only what a required output depends on.

## Example scenario

A neutral baseline of 15 to 20 minutes. Replace `[technology]` and the bracketed wording with your own.

````md
# Professional Practice and Emerging Technology

## Purpose
Understand how a participant currently uses [technology] in their own work,
across their team, and with customers. Identify concrete uses, demonstrated
value, limitations, barriers, and the main opportunity for the next six months.

## Required outputs
- Participant role and work context.
- At least one concrete example of current use, or confirmation of non-use.
- Observed value, limitations and decision criteria.
- Main barrier or unmet need.
- One prioritised opportunity and a plausible first step.

## Flow

### Introduction
- {{ contact.greeting }}
- "Thanks for making the time. I'm Aidan, reaching out on behalf of [sponsor]."
- State the goal of the discussion.
- State how the interview will be used, and that the participant can ask for
  the discussion to remain confidential at any point. Ask if all is clear.

### 1. Role and current context
Objective: establish responsibilities and current focus.
- "What responsibilities are taking most of your attention at the moment?"
Evidence: role, current priorities, team context.

### 2. Individual practice
Objective: understand actual behaviour, value and limitations.
- "Where, if anywhere, does [technology] appear in your own work today?"
- "Please walk me through a recent example."
- "What changed as a result: time, quality, cost, risk?"
- "Where has it been unreliable or unsuitable?"
If the participant does not use it:
- "What has prevented or discouraged you from using it?"
Evidence: activity and frequency, observable outcome, reason for non-use.

### 3. Team practice
Ask only if the participant has visibility into a team.
- "How, if at all, is [technology] used across your team?"
- "What appears to help or hinder effective use?"

### 4. Barriers and opportunity
- "Where is the largest unresolved need or opportunity?"
- "What is currently blocking progress?"
- "If only one change could be made in the next six months, what should it be?"
Evidence: one prioritised opportunity, principal barrier, first step.

### 5. Conclusion
- Summarise two or three findings for confirmation.
- "What important point have we not covered?"

## Branches
- **No direct use:** focus on reasons and conditions for trial, then go to
  Team practice.
- **No team visibility:** skip Team practice.
- **Insufficient time:** Individual practice, Barriers and opportunity,
  Conclusion.

## Completion criteria
The interview is complete when each required output is supported by concrete
evidence or explicitly recorded as unknown or not applicable.
````

## Final review

- [ ] Does the purpose name the decision the interview supports?
- [ ] Is every question tied to a required output?
- [ ] Does the flow move from facts toward interpretation and prioritisation?
- [ ] Can every branch rejoin the main path?
- [ ] Is the scope realistic for the stated duration, and tested with short, typical and long answers?
- [ ] Is every piece of participant context either loaded or asked for?
- [ ] Have tone and behaviour instructions been removed?
- [ ] Will the outputs be comparable across interviews?
